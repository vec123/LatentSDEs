Bayesian Shrinkage: Deep Dive

In Bayesian shrinkage, we define the shrinkage factor κi, which acts as a weight: βposterior = (1 - κi)β.

Fundamental Relationship:

κi = 1 / (1 + λi2)

  • If λi is large (Signal): κi → 0 (No shrinkage).
  • If λi is small (Noise): κi → 1 (Shrink toward zero).

Comparison of Prior Mechanics

Prior Local Scale (λi) Shrinkage Density Behavior
Gaussian Fixed/Constant None Global; no sparsity.
Laplace Exponential High near 1 Biases large signals.
Student-t Inverse-Gamma Adaptive Robust; lacks sparsity.
Horseshoe Half-Cauchy Singularities at 0 & 1 Optimal; all-or-nothing.

Hierarchical Structure of the Horseshoe

The Horseshoe succeeds by using a two-level hierarchy:

  1. Likelihood: βi ~ N(0, λi2τ2)
  2. Local Shrinkage: λi ~ Half-Cauchy(0, 1)
  3. Global Shrinkage: τ ~ Half-Cauchy(0, τ0)

The Half-Cauchy prior is the "secret sauce." Its extremely heavy tails allow λi to grow very large for true signals (letting the signal pass through), while its infinite spike at zero ensures that noise is effectively crushed.
